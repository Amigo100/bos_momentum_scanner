Market Environment & Structural Landscape

V6.0
ModeExtended Thinking + Web Search
Time~10-12 min
ProcessSingle prompt. Landscape mapping before tickers.
Before I give you this week's scanner signals, I need you to map the current
market environment and structural landscape. This context will serve as a
REFERENCE FRAME for all downstream analysis — but it must NOT predetermine
which stocks or themes we favour. Use your full search budget here.

Our hold period is 3-18 months. Assess everything with that lens. We care about
what the environment looks like over the next 6-18 months, not what happened
this week. Short-term volatility is noise; structural regime shifts are signal.

You already have the retrospective calibration notes from Prompt 1 in this
conversation. Keep those in mind — particularly if recent outcomes suggest
adjusting how aggressively we deploy capital or filter themes.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PART 1: MARKET REGIME — 6-18 MONTH STRUCTURAL OUTLOOK
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Time budget: ~3 minutes. Focus on STRUCTURAL TRAJECTORY, not weekly noise.

Search: "stock market outlook economy growth forecast 2026 2027"
Search: "Fed interest rate path expectations 2026 2027"
Search: "S&P 500 earnings growth forecast next 12 months 2026"
Search: "small cap Russell 2000 relative performance trend 2026"

We're NOT asking "is the market up or down this week?" We're asking: "Is the
environment over the next 6-18 months supportive of small-cap growth stocks
breaking out and sustaining multi-month runs?"

REGIME CLASSIFICATION:

- RISK-ON: Economic expansion, accommodative or neutral policy, earnings growth
  accelerating, credit conditions supportive, liquidity ample.
  → Our momentum system works best here. Small caps can sustain multi-month
  trends. The macro tide lifts breakouts rather than drowning them.
  Deploy capital at normal levels.

- RISK-OFF: Recession risk elevated, tightening policy, earnings contracting,
  credit stress, flight to safety.
  → Momentum signals are less reliable. Breakouts fail more often as macro
  headwinds overpower individual stock catalysts. Even good thematic setups
  get dragged down. Tighten all filters, reduce total deployment.

- SELECTIVE: Mixed environment — some sectors expanding, others contracting.
  Leadership narrow. Rotation active. Policy crosscurrents.
  → Theme selection matters MORE than usual. The right themes can still
  produce monster winners, but scattered deployment across weak themes
  underperforms. We need to be in the strongest themes, not just any theme.

KEY DATA POINTS (focus on TREND and DIRECTION across quarters, not snapshots):

a) ECONOMIC TRAJECTORY:
   - GDP growth trend (accelerating / stable / decelerating)
   - Employment conditions (tightening / stable / loosening)
   - Consumer spending (resilient / weakening)
   - Is the economy EARLY-CYCLE (best for small caps), MID-CYCLE (good),
     or LATE-CYCLE (risk rising)?

b) MONETARY POLICY & LIQUIDITY:
   - Fed funds rate: current level and expected path for next 12 months
   - Real rates: Are they restrictive or neutral?
   - Liquidity conditions: Fed balance sheet direction, reverse repo, bank
     reserves. Ample liquidity lifts ALL boats; tightening liquidity
     collapses the weakest themes first.
   - Any upcoming FOMC meetings, CPI prints, or jobs reports that could
     shift the rate path?

c) EARNINGS CYCLE:
   - S&P 500 earnings growth: current quarter and forward 4 quarters
   - CRITICALLY: Is earnings growth BROADENING beyond mega-caps? When
     only the top 7 stocks drive earnings growth, small caps lag. When
     the other 493 start growing earnings, capital rotates to small caps.
     This broadening is the single most important data point for our system.
   - Small-cap earnings trajectory specifically (Russell 2000 earnings growth)

d) CREDIT CONDITIONS:
   - High-yield spreads (tight = risk-on, widening = stress)
   - Bank lending standards (loosening / tightening)
   - VC/IPO activity — is private capital flowing into growth companies?
     Active IPO markets signal risk appetite for the type of stocks we trade.

e) SMALL-CAP STRUCTURAL ENVIRONMENT:
   Search: "small cap vs large cap relative performance rotation 2026"
   Search: "small cap growth ETF flows institutional allocation"
   - IWM relative performance vs SPY over 3M/6M (STRUCTURAL trend, not weekly)
   - Is capital STRUCTURALLY flowing into or out of small caps?
   - Are institutional allocators rotating toward small caps? Any notable
     research reports or allocation shifts calling for small-cap outperformance?
   - Our system lives here. If small caps are in a sustained multi-month
     drawdown relative to large caps, even our best signals face headwinds
     over the hold period.

f) REGIME-CHANGING EVENTS ON THE HORIZON:
   - Geopolitical risks that could shift the regime within 3-6 months
   - Trade policy changes (tariffs, sanctions, trade agreements)
   - Regulatory shifts (sector-specific deregulation or new regulation)
   - Elections or policy uncertainty windows
   - Any of these that could SHIFT the regime classification during a
     typical 3-18 month hold? If yes, flag as a tail risk.

PART 2: CAPITAL FLOW MAP — WHERE MONEY IS MOVING
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Time budget: ~3 minutes. Map where institutional capital is flowing NOW
and where it's ROTATING TOWARD. This is NOT about which sectors were hot
last month — it's about where the flow is ACCELERATING.

Search: "sector ETF flows capital rotation trends 2026"
Search: "which sectors leading market performance 3 month 6 month 2026"
Search: "institutional capital allocation trends sectors 2026"

a) SECTOR MOMENTUM MAP:
   Search: "sector performance ranking XLK XLV XLF XLE XLI XLU XLB 2026"

   Classify each major sector into one of:
   - STRUCTURAL UPTREND: 3M AND 6M performance positive and accelerating.
     Capital is flowing in and INCREASING. These sectors have wind at their
     backs — themes within them have macro support for sustained runs.
   - STABLE: Performance positive but not accelerating. Capital is steady.
     Themes here can work but need strong individual catalysts.
   - STRUCTURAL DOWNTREND: 3M AND/OR 6M performance negative or decelerating.
     Capital is rotating OUT. Themes here face headwinds — even good
     micro-themes may underperform as the sector tide goes out.

   Present as a compact map:
   ACCELERATING: [Sector 1, Sector 2] — capital flowing in, momentum building
   STABLE: [Sector 3, Sector 4] — holding, no strong directional flow
   DECELERATING: [Sector 5, Sector 6] — capital rotating out, headwind

b) ESTABLISHED THEMATIC WAVES:
   Search: "market themes driving stock performance 2026"
   Search: "hot investment themes multiple stocks breaking out"

   These are themes the MARKET ALREADY KNOWS ABOUT — where multiple stocks
   have produced sustained multi-month breakouts on the SAME structural driver.

   IMPORTANT: Name SPECIFIC structural drivers, not sector labels.
   - GOOD: "Data centre power infrastructure buildout driven by AI electricity
     demand" — tells you exactly what's driving the wave
   - BAD: "Technology stocks are doing well" — meaningless sector label

   For each established wave:
   - Theme name (specific structural driver, not sector label)
   - Duration so far (when did this wave start producing breakouts?)
   - Participating stocks (which names have already moved significantly?)

   - DUAL LIFECYCLE ASSESSMENT (V6):
     Position this wave on BOTH frameworks. They measure different things
     and the combination is the investment signal.

     HYPE CYCLE position (measures EXPECTATIONS):
       a) Innovation Trigger — first visibility, no proven products yet
       b) Peak of Inflated Expectations — frenzy, overinvestment, all stocks rally
       c) Trough of Disillusionment — reality check, failures, stocks sold off
       d) Slope of Enlightenment — survivors prove model, adoption rises steadily
       e) Plateau of Productivity — mainstream adoption, returns normalise

     S-CURVE position (measures ACTUAL ADOPTION):
       a) Pre-inflection (<10% market penetration) — works but not yet scaled
       b) Inflection point (10-30% penetration) — adoption accelerating exponentially
       c) Rapid growth (30-60% penetration) — mainstream, growth visible but slowing
       d) Mature (>60% penetration) — saturated, incremental growth only

     MULTIBAGGER SWEET SPOT FLAG:
     The highest-return investment setups occur when the S-curve hits inflection
     (10-30% adoption) WHILE the hype cycle is at Trough or early Slope. This
     combination means: actual adoption is accelerating but sentiment and prices
     are beaten down. Survivors are undervalued relative to reality.
     Examples: AI in late 2022 (S-curve inflection + post-crypto-winter sentiment
     trough). Quantum computing in mid-2024 (S-curve pre-inflection + first
     commercial milestones + low expectations).

     If BOTH conditions are met, flag:
     "SWEET SPOT: S-curve inflection + sentiment trough. This combination
     precedes multibagger waves."

     If S-curve is at inflection but hype cycle is at Peak, flag:
     "HIGH GROWTH BUT ELEVATED EXPECTATIONS — late entrants face re-rating
     risk if any catalyst disappoints. New positions need strong individual merit."

   - NEW ENTRANT POTENTIAL: Given the lifecycle position, can a NEW small-cap
     signal in this wave still produce 3x+ returns over 18 months?
     • S-curve pre-inflection or inflection: YES — the main move is still ahead
     • S-curve rapid growth + hype Slope: POSSIBLE — strong companies only
     • S-curve mature or hype Peak: UNLIKELY — most gains captured
     • Hype Trough + S-curve inflection: HIGHEST POTENTIAL — asymmetric setup

   Typically 2-4 established waves are active at any time. If you identify more
   than 5, you're probably labelling sector performance as waves — be stricter
   about requiring SPECIFIC structural drivers with MULTIPLE co-moving stocks.

   If none detected: "No powerful multi-stock waves currently active. Market
   leadership is dispersed across sectors without a dominant thematic driver."

c) SMALL-CAP FLOW DYNAMICS:
   Search: "small cap growth capital flows Russell 2000 2026"
   - Net flow direction into/out of small-cap growth ETFs (IWO, VBK, etc.)
   - Any institutional rotation calls (research reports recommending small-cap
     overweight, pension fund allocation shifts)
   - Retail sentiment toward small caps (bullish / neutral / bearish)
   - This tells us whether the ASSET CLASS our system trades in has a tailwind
     or headwind — independent of any specific theme.

PART 3: STRUCTURAL SHIFTS & EMERGING FORCES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

THIS IS THE HIGHEST-VALUE SECTION. Allocate the most search budget and
deepest thinking time here. Spend at least 4-5 minutes.

These are structural changes in the economy, technology, policy, or markets
that are CREATING new investment opportunities. They may or may not have
produced visible stock market winners yet. The first stock in a new wave
always looks like a standalone bet — finding the wave BEFORE broad momentum is
visible is how our system catches 200-700% winners.

The distinction from Part 2: Established Waves are things the market already
knows about. Emerging Forces are things that are STRUCTURALLY happening but
NOT YET reflected in broad stock market momentum. The wave hasn't started yet,
or it's just beginning.

Search BROADLY across multiple dimensions. Cast a wide net — you're looking
for structural forces, not just stock market themes:

Search: "emerging technology adoption curves inflection point 2026"
Search: "government policy creating growth sectors spending programs 2026"
Search: "supply demand imbalances creating investment opportunity 2026"
Search: "venture capital new investment areas early stage 2026 2027"
Search: "structural economic shifts disruption 2026 2027"

Think across FIVE force categories:

a) TECHNOLOGY TRANSITIONS:
   What technologies are moving from experimental to commercial deployment?
   What's reaching the tipping point where adoption accelerates exponentially?
   Look for: Technologies where unit economics just became viable, where the
   first major enterprise customer was just signed, where government funding
   was just approved, or where a regulatory pathway was just cleared.
   (Examples of past winners: AI inference in 2023, quantum computing milestones
   in 2024, mRNA platform technology post-COVID)

   For each emerging force, also assess the DUAL LIFECYCLE POSITION (V6):
   - Hype Cycle: Almost always Innovation Trigger or early Peak. But check:
     is this force RETURNING from a prior Trough? (Hydrogen in 2023 re-emerging
     after the 2021 bubble burst is a repeat cycle with different dynamics.)
   - S-Curve: Almost always pre-inflection (<10%) for genuine emerging forces.
     If adoption is already at 10-30%, it may be an established wave that Part 2
     missed. Reclassify.
   - Timeline to Sweet Spot: How many months/years until the S-curve reaches
     inflection? If >24 months, too early for our 6-18 month timeframe (note
     for future sessions). If 6-18 months, flag as APPROACHING — the wave may
     start during our hold period.


b) POLICY & REGULATORY CATALYSTS:
   What government actions are CREATING winners — either through direct spending,
   regulatory change, or strategic prioritisation?
   Look for: New legislation with specific budget allocations, executive orders
   directing procurement, deregulation opening previously restricted markets,
   trade policy creating domestic demand (tariffs, reshoring mandates, export
   restrictions that benefit domestic players).
   (Examples: CHIPS Act → domestic semiconductor manufacturing, defense budget
   expansion → autonomous systems, IRA → clean energy deployment)

c) SUPPLY-DEMAND IMBALANCES:
   Where does demand structurally and persistently exceed supply?
   Supply constraints create pricing power, which creates earnings growth,
   which creates stock re-ratings. The best supply-demand themes persist for
   years because expanding supply takes time (building factories, training
   workforce, permitting).
   Look for: Multi-year demand ramps meeting constrained supply (not temporary
   shortages). Where are companies raising prices because they CAN, not because
   costs went up?
   (Examples: Data centre power demand vs grid capacity, skilled trades labour
   shortage, critical minerals supply deficit)

d) SECULAR DEMAND SHIFTS:
   What consumer, enterprise, or government behaviours are structurally changing
   in ways that create multi-year demand for specific products or services?
   Not temporary trends — permanent shifts in how money gets spent.
   Look for: Demographic forces (aging populations, urbanisation), healthcare
   delivery model changes, payment system transitions, cybersecurity necessity,
   infrastructure modernisation requirements.
   (Examples: Healthcare moving to outpatient/ambulatory, enterprise AI workflow
   integration, infrastructure replacement cycles)

e) CAPITAL CYCLE TURNS:
   What sectors experienced over-investment, shook out weak players through a
   bear market, and are now at the early stage of recovery?
   Capital cycle recoveries are powerful because: (1) supply has been destroyed
   by the downturn, (2) survivors have pricing power, (3) institutional capital
   re-enters as fundamentals improve, and (4) the market has been burned and
   is slow to recognise the turn — which gives us entry before consensus.
   Look for: Sectors where the last 2-3 years were brutal, multiple bankruptcies
   or exits occurred, but underlying demand is now recovering.
   (Examples: Uranium sector recovery in 2023, biotech after 2022-23 bear market,
   offshore drilling recovery, specific commodity cycles)

For each identified structural force:
1. SPECIFIC NAME: Not a sector label. A named structural shift you could write
   a 2-sentence thesis about.
2. THE DRIVER: What changed and why it can't easily reverse? (Policy, technology
   tipping point, demographic force, supply destruction + demand recovery)
3. EARLY EVIDENCE: At least one concrete signal — specific dollar amounts, deal
   names, regulatory filings, corporate capex commitments. "VC funding is
   increasing in this space" is weak. "$2.1B in Series B/C rounds for drone
   companies in Q4 2025, up 340% YoY" is strong.
4. MAGNITUDE: Is this a $10B market creation or a $500B+ structural shift?
   Bigger structural shifts produce more winners and sustain longer runs.
5. TIMING: How early are we?
   - PRE-BREAKOUT: The structural force exists but hasn't produced visible stock
     market winners yet. Highest potential but longest wait. We might see scanner
     signals in 4-12 weeks if the thesis is right.
   - FIRST MOVERS: 1-3 stocks have started moving but no broad recognition yet.
     This is where our scanner might catch the first signal in the wave.
   - EARLY ADOPTION: Multiple stocks moving, first analyst coverage appearing,
     sector ETFs beginning to see inflows. Still early enough for 3-5x returns.
   - BROAD ADOPTION: Consensus forming, heavy coverage, ETF inflows established.
     Overlaps with "established waves" from Part 2. Late for new entry.
6. DURATION: Is this a 6-month trade, a 2-year theme, or a decade-long secular
   shift? Our system needs 12+ months of runway to produce monster winners.
7. SMALL-CAP SIGNAL: What type of small-cap stock would show up in our scanner
   if this force is producing breakouts? (E.g., "Small-cap drone manufacturers,
   autonomous systems software companies, defence tech hardware suppliers under
   $2B market cap.") This helps pattern-match when we see scanner signals later.

Aim for 3-6 structural forces. Quality over quantity — each should have
concrete evidence, not just a thesis. If you can only find 2 with real evidence,
that's better than padding with 5 speculative ideas.

If none detected with concrete evidence: "No clear emerging structural forces
identified with measurable evidence this week. The opportunity landscape hasn't
changed materially from last session." This is a valid and honest answer.

⚠️ HOW THIS CONTEXT IS USED DOWNSTREAM (READ CAREFULLY)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

This landscape assessment has FOUR specific roles in the pipeline.
Understanding these prevents both over-use (top-down filtering) and
under-use (ignoring valuable context):

1. REGIME → DEPLOYMENT STANCE (affects Prompt 4 and Prompt 5)
   The regime classification determines how aggressively we deploy capital.
   RISK-ON: standard thresholds.
   SELECTIVE: themes need to be stronger to justify deployment.
   RISK-OFF: only exceptional themes with exceptional stocks.
   This is a THRESHOLD adjustment, not a filter. Every theme still gets
   analysed on its own merits — the regime determines how HIGH the bar is.

2. ESTABLISHED WAVES → WAVE STRENGTH CONTEXT (used in Prompt 4)
   When Prompt 3's bottom-up discovery maps signals to themes, and one
   of those themes matches an established wave or structural force
   identified here, that's STRUCTURAL FORCE ALIGNMENT — indicating the
   stock rides a funded capital wave. Prompt 4 uses this alongside capital
   flow evidence from Prompt 2B to assess wave strength.
   BUT: Absence of alignment is NOT a negative signal. The most valuable
   discoveries may be themes too early for macro analysis to catch. A
   bottom-up theme with no Prompt 2 match may be the first signal in an
   emerging wave — which is exactly what produced our best historical winners.

3. STRUCTURAL FORCES → CANDIDATE IDENTIFICATION (used in Prompt 2B)
   Prompt 2B takes the structural forces from Part 3 and assesses which
   have investable small-cap vehicles. For forces scoring 3+ on
   investability, Prompt 2B identifies 3-5 candidate stocks per force.
   These candidates are fed into Prompt 3 alongside scanner signals,
   receiving the SAME rigorous bottom-up analysis. The macro overlay
   ensures our pipeline SEES stocks riding the most powerful capital
   flows, even if our scanner hasn't caught them yet.

4. STRUCTURAL FORCES → THEME DEPTH REFERENCE (used in Prompt 4)
   When Prompt 4 evaluates the quality of a bottom-up theme, it checks:
