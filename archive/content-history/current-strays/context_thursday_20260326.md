## SUPPORTING DATA

### Research Concept

Cash floor analysis calculates the minimum liquidation value of a
company's balance sheet to establish a known boundary on potential
downside. For pre-revenue or early-stage companies, this transforms
an open-ended risk into a bounded one.

The framework:
1. Calculate net cash per share (cash + short-term investments - total debt)
2. Add near-term receivables or contracted revenue (collaboration payments,
   milestones already earned)
3. Subtract estimated cash burn over the analysis horizon (6-12 months)
4. The result is the "cash floor": the price at which you are effectively
   buying the company's assets at liquidation value with zero premium for
   the business

When the stock price approaches or sits near the cash floor, the
risk/reward profile becomes asymmetric: downside is structurally bounded
while upside depends on business execution and catalysts.

### Portfolio Example: VYGR

- Entry price: $4.45
- Cash position: $201.7M
- Shares outstanding: 55.6M
- Cash per share: $3.63
- With collaboration reimbursements and interest income, effective floor:
  $3.43-3.76 over 12 months
- Maximum realistic downside from entry: 18-20% (to cash floor)
- Weighted upside target: $8.40 (+89%)
- Risk/reward ratio: approximately 4.5:1
- Stop price set at $3.40 (just below cash floor)

This is a real position in our portfolio entered March 18 at $4.45.
The cash floor analysis was a critical input to the position sizing
decision (T2, 10% of equity) and the stop placement ($3.40).

### How Cash Floor Analysis Protected Capital: Historical Example

When evaluating DDD (Defence Additive Manufacturing) in the March 14
scan, the cash floor analysis produced a very different result:
- Cash: $97M
- Shares outstanding: 189M fully diluted (after 45% dilution in 2 years)
- Cash per share: $0.51
- Annual cash burn: $73M
- 12-month cash floor: approximately $0.13/share
- Stock price at scan: $2.42
- Downside to cash floor: 95%

The cash floor analysis revealed that DDD's "cheap" stock price at $2.42
was actually expensive relative to its liquidation value. The position
was rejected at the forensic research stage.

### Broader Application

Cash floor analysis is most useful for:
1. Pre-revenue biotech with partnership income (like VYGR)
2. Asset-heavy companies with identifiable liquidation values
3. Companies with large cash positions relative to market cap
4. Post-SPAC companies with trust residuals

It is least useful for:
1. Companies with significant debt that would take priority in liquidation
2. Companies with high fixed costs that accelerate cash burn in distress
3. Companies where "cash" includes restricted or committed capital

### System Integration

Our screening system uses cash floor analysis at Stage 5 (Deep Due
Diligence) as one of several downside protection checks. The cash floor
informs:
- Stop placement: stops are set just below the cash floor when the floor
  is identifiable
- Position sizing: positions with high cash floors relative to entry price
  can justify larger allocations because the maximum loss is bounded
- Risk/reward calculation: the denominator of the risk/reward ratio uses
  the cash floor as the realistic worst case, not zero

### Key Takeaway for Readers

Most retail investors evaluate downside as "the stock could go to zero."
Cash floor analysis replaces that assumption with a specific number based
on balance sheet data. The difference between "this could go to zero" and
"this could go to $3.43" fundamentally changes how you size the position
and where you place your stop.
