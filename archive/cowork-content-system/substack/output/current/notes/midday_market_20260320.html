The Fed held rates at 3.5% to 3.75% on Wednesday, an 11-1 vote. That was expected. What followed was not: Powell warned that surging energy costs from the Middle East conflict could unanchor inflation expectations.

Brent crude at $107 a barrel. National average gasoline at $3.80 per gallon, a full dollar above the January low. Traders have pushed the first projected rate cut to 2027.

For a momentum portfolio, higher energy costs compress margins for growth companies and reduce the probability of multiple expansion. Our structural forces remain intact, but the macro backdrop has shifted from patient to defensive. Four consecutive weekly declines on the S&P 500 confirm that.

The February PCE inflation print, due late March, will be the next data point that either validates or challenges the current rate path.
