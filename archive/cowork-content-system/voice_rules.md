## 1. Core Voice

Sterling Signals writes like a research desk briefing a portfolio manager.
Data-first. Direct. Opinionated but evidence-backed. The system is the
authority, not the author.

Every sentence either presents a fact, draws a conclusion from facts, or
states what happens next. If a sentence does none of these, cut it.

## 2. The 15 Rules

### Data and precision

**Rule 1: Open with data or a concrete statement.**
Never open with greetings, preamble, or throat-clearing. The first sentence
of every post and every note contains either a number or a specific claim.

Good: "1,817 stocks entered the screening system this week. One survived."
Bad: "Welcome back to another edition of Sterling Signals."

**Rule 2: $TICKER at $PRICE. Always this format.**
When referencing a specific position, lead with the ticker and price.
Bold the ticker in HTML content. Use the dollar sign prefix.

Good: "$TMDX at $65.00. Now $121.31."
Bad: "TransMedics, which we bought at sixty-five dollars..."

**Rule 3: Specific numbers always.**
Never use vague qualifiers. Every claim about growth, valuation, or
performance must include the actual number.

Good: "Revenue grew 21% YoY to $874M."
Bad: "Revenue showed strong growth."
Good: "Trading at 0.7x forward revenue."
Bad: "Trading at a cheap valuation."

### Authority and framing

**Rule 4: The system is the authority.**
Use "our system," "the screening," "our research process," "the filter
chain." Never "I think," "I believe," "in my opinion." The system
produces conclusions. We report them.

Good: "The screening system flagged 11 signals. One cleared the Investment Gate."
Bad: "I found a really interesting stock this week."

**Rule 5: No AI or LLM references.**
Frame all analysis as "deep fundamental research," "forensic analysis,"
"proprietary screening process," "our research methodology." Never
mention Claude, AI-powered analysis, LLM, machine learning, or any
tool that a subscriber could replicate.

Good: "Our forensic analysis uncovered 45% dilution over two years."
Bad: "Our AI-powered research tool identified capital structure issues."

**Rule 6: Never reveal specific technical indicators.**
The scanner's indicators (HMA, MACD, Banker, MCDX, RSI, UC, Undercurrent)
are proprietary. Describe what the system detects, not how.

Good: "momentum inflection," "trend reversal confirmed," "institutional
accumulation building," "five-stage filter chain"
Bad: "HMA pivot low detected," "MACD crossed above zero"

**Rule 7: Reference structural forces, not micro themes.**
Public content maps positions to the seven macro structural forces, not
to the scanner's internal micro-theme classifications.

The seven forces: Defence Spending, Nuclear Renaissance, Biotech Capital
Cycle, AI Infrastructure, Grid Modernisation, Cybersecurity Compliance,
Financial Infrastructure.

Good: "$ASPI sits within the Nuclear Renaissance force. SWU prices have
tripled to $188 since 2022."
Bad: "$ASPI's theme is Nuclear Fuel with a composite score of 7.2/10."

### Transparency

**Rule 8: Show rejected stocks and explain why.**
Rejection stories build more trust than winner highlights. When the system
says no, explain the reasoning. This teaches subscribers and demonstrates
the rigour of the screening process.

Good: "We walked away from a 3D printing company with a defence tailwind.
The capital structure told a different story: 45% dilution in two years."
Bad: (Omitting rejections entirely)

**Rule 9: Show losses alongside wins.**
Never hide underperforming positions. State facts about negative P&L
without using emotionally loaded language.

Good: "$EVTL at $4.50. Now $3.82. The thesis remains intact but needs
validation from the March 24 earnings report."
Bad: "$EVTL is bleeding out" / (hiding the position from the update)

### Formatting and rhythm

**Rule 10: No exclamation marks. Ever.**
Not in posts. Not in notes. Not in any Sterling Signals content.

**Rule 11: No hype language.**

BANNED: explosive, massive, incredible, game-changer, moon, rocket,
skyrocket, crushing it, killing it, insane, wild, fire (as adjective),
"let's dive in," "here's the thing," "it's worth noting," "without
further ado," "buckle up"

APPROVED: asymmetric, structural, systematic, conviction, forensic,
disciplined, selective, proportional

**Rule 12: Colons and periods, not dashes.**
Em dashes are an LLM fingerprint. Replace with colons for elaboration,
periods for separation, semicolons for closely related clauses, or
restructured sentences. Occasional use of a single hyphen for compound
modifiers is fine (rate-sensitive, pre-revenue).

Bad: "The thesis is straightforward — buy quality companies at inflection points — and the data supports it."
Good: "The thesis is straightforward: buy quality companies at inflection points. The data supports it."

**Rule 13: Vary sentence length deliberately.**
Mix short declarative sentences (6-10 words) with longer analytical
ones (20-30 words). Break LLM patterns by avoiding:
- Triple parallel constructions ("X matters. Y matters. Z matters.")
- Formulaic list openings repeated across sections
- Predictable sentence-length uniformity

Good paragraph rhythm: Short. Medium with detail and context that builds
the argument. Short again. Then a longer sentence that brings the analysis
together before landing on a conclusion.

**Rule 14: Table of contents for posts over 1,000 words.**
Place the key actionable insight toward the end of the post to pull
readers through the full piece. The most important information for a
subscriber (new entries, exit decisions, forward catalysts) goes in
the final third.

**Rule 15: End with what happens next.**
Posts and notes end with forward-looking content: the next catalyst,
the next event, what the system is watching. Never end with personal
sign-offs, requests for likes, or generic encouragement.

Good: "Q1 earnings in late April will either validate or challenge
this thesis. We are watching Enterprise Voice growth above 20%."
Bad: "Thanks for reading. Hit subscribe if you found this useful."

## 3. Banned Terms Registry

### Indicator names (NEVER in public content)

HMA, Hull Moving Average, MACD, RSI, Banker, UC, Undercurrent, MCDX,
KDJ, trailing stop (as a named system), "Banker indicator," "upper
channel," "ExD exit," "pivot low," "pivot high"

### Approved replacements for indicator language

| Internal term | Public replacement |
|---|---|
| HMA pivot low | "momentum inflection," "trend reversal confirmed" |
| MACD cross-up | "trend acceleration confirmed" |
| Banker/UC rising | "institutional accumulation signal" |
| ExD exit signal | "systematic exit triggered" |
| Buy signal | "cleared our screening filters" |
| T1/T2/T3 | "Tier 1 (highest conviction)" / "Tier 2" / "Tier 3 (smallest allocation)" |
| PASS/CONSIDER | "GREEN signal" / "watchlisted" |
| Gate verdict | "cleared the Investment Gate" |
| Theme score 7.6/10 | Do not reference numerical scores. Use force status: READY/APPROACHING |

### Marketing language (NEVER use)

"Let's dive in," "Here's the thing," "It's worth noting," "Without
further ado," "Buckle up," "Not financial advice but...," "This is
not financial advice, however..."

### Regional/personal terms (NEVER use)

UK ISA, GMT, BST, Roth IRA (unless explicitly discussing tax strategy),
PDT rule, 401k, "mate," "cheers," any British colloquialisms

### Negative P&L framing (NEVER use)

"-X%," "down X%," "loss," "losing," "stopped out," "bleeding," "worst
performer," "red," "in the red," "underwater"

### Approved negative P&L language

| Instead of... | Use... |
|---|---|
| "down 15%" | "$EVTL at $4.50. Now $3.82." (let the reader do the maths, or state: "That's -15.1% from entry.") |
| "our worst performer" | "our weakest holding" |
| "stopped out" | "systematic exit triggered" or "exit criteria met" |
| "loss" | "drawdown" or state the factual P&L change |
| "bleeding" | (describe the price movement factually) |

## 5. Formatting Standards

### Posts (HTML)

- Font family: DM Sans (body), DM Serif Display (headings)
- Max width: 780px
- Colour palette: navy #0a1628, blue #1a3a5c / #2563eb, slate #334155 / #64748b, green #16a34a, red #dc2626, amber #d97706
- Tables: styled with header row, alternating row shading, monospace for numbers
- Stat grids: flexbox row with key metrics at the top of the post
- Every post includes: table of contents (linked), preview line (1-2 sentences for email/feed), disclaimer footer

### Notes (plain text for Substack Notes)

- 50-150 words
- Line breaks for emphasis and readability
- $TICKER always referenced with dollar sign
- No HTML formatting (Substack Notes are plain text with basic markdown)
- End with: factual statement, forward look, or call to read the full post
- Disclaimer line: "Not financial advice."

### Visual cards (680px HTML, screenshot to PNG)

- Dark header (#0a1628)
- Stat boxes in #0f2440
- Green #16a34a for positive, red #dc2626 for negative
- Body text #1a1a1a, muted #6b6b6b
- Brand footer: "STERLING SIGNALS" + "Not financial advice"

## 6. Content Quality Gate

Before any piece of content is published, check:

1. Does the first sentence contain a number or specific claim?
2. Are all P&L figures calculated from actual entry prices, not estimated?
3. Are any technical indicator names exposed? (If yes: rewrite.)
4. Are positions mapped to structural forces, not micro themes?
5. Does every claim have a specific number attached?
6. Are there any em dashes? (If yes: replace with colons/periods.)
7. Are there any AI/LLM references? (If yes: reframe.)
8. Does the piece end with a forward-looking statement?
9. Would a subscriber learn something or gain a data point they didn't have?
10. Is there at least one rejection story or honest loser assessment? (for posts)
