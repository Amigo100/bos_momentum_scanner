1. The Headline

The book now holds ten positions averaging 65.0%, one of them up 336.3% since entry and three up more than 80%, yet across the same week the screen ran 57 fresh candidates and issued zero GREEN signals.

The growth and the zero belong together, because together they describe how this book is built: it expands when we choose to add a name, not when a screen tells us to be busy. The book grew by two last week, on our own decision, and the weekly screen this week added nothing at all. Both moves were deliberate. BAND sits at 72.20 against a 16.55 entry; SVCO, HIVE, CYRX, ASPI, VOYG and ACRS are all green; ten names in total, seven of them in profit, three carried below entry and named as such. Two of those ten, TEM in artificial-intelligence healthcare and CRSP in gene editing, were added last week at the penultimate weekly candle close, outside the weekly screen, and we walk through both in Section 3. Into that book, this week's screen took one new name all the way through full due diligence and still issued no GREEN signal.

The distinction matters, so we draw it sharply. The two additions came from our own conviction on names we wanted exposure to, carrying street-consensus targets rather than our own completed due diligence. The weekly screen, the systematic process that has built the rest of this book, produced a clean zero from 57 candidates. We would rather report that honestly than blur the two. The backdrop sharpened the case for the screen sitting still: the policy-easing turn slipped toward hawkish on a hot jobs print, the small-cap rotation bent rather than broke, and the most fragile balance sheets picked up a marginal headwind. Two data points ahead, both in Section 5, decide whether the screen's door swings wider. Until then, the portfolio is where the story is.

Eighteen names reached the deep-dive review, counting five we deliberately reinstated; one cleared it and completed full due diligence, none earned capital. Here are the rejections that teach the most, each tagged to the stage where it failed.

CNM First triage · false exposure

It surfaced on the artificial-intelligence power-and-grid screen, exactly the label that earns a fast pass somewhere less disciplined. The revenue is water and wastewater infrastructure, with zero data-center power. The match was the word, not the business.

The lessonWe do not buy a label. A name on a hot-force screen still has to earn the exposure in its actual revenue mix, and a zero mix ends it.
GXAI Verification · peripheral exposure

A counter-drone story in our single strongest unowned force. Verification found the entire exposure was a 19.99% minority stake in an early-stage company that merely licenses the technology: no funded program, no firm order, no defense revenue on its own books.

The lessonPeripheral exposure to a hot force is still a pass. Owning a sliver of a company that licenses the thing is not owning it.
AIRO Deep-dive review · inverted evidence

The hardest to pass on: a genuine counter-drone pure-play in the unowned force we most want to add, and it earned a deep-dive slot on the strength of the story. Then the load-bearing evidence ran backwards. The order book has declined every quarter since the company came public, and the thesis rests entirely on converting a shrinking backlog into rising revenue.

The lessonA shrinking order book is the classic round-tripper signature. What separates a thematic winner from one that runs and gives it all back is whether the operational evidence is accelerating or fading.
BNED · STRT · TGLS · BXC · YXT Deep-dive review · reinstated, re-dropped

The most revealing decision of the week, because we deliberately reinstated all five in case the first cut had been too quick. On current data, the verdict held: four capped near two times, short of the asymmetry bar, and one tripped the survival firewall. The wide recall net gave them a hearing; the hard bar still refused them.

The lessonRecall and precision are two different jobs. Cast a wide net early so a real shot never dies on a technicality, then let a hard bar refuse anything short of the threshold.

What looked good. The only year-long implantable glucose monitor on the market reclaimed its own commercial channel effective January 1, a single move that stepped gross margin to 59% by letting the company book revenue it previously split, and removed the distribution dependency that broke this same story in 2019. The product cycle is demonstrably live: revenue grew 87% year over year, the second straight quarter of acceleration, management raised full-year guidance, and the European launch began executing. It is taking share in a 15 billion-dollar market two incumbents have held locked at about 98%.

Why it still said no. Against a deliberately sharpened opportunity bar, only the margin step is shown today. The load-bearing growth leg, automated-insulin-delivery integration, is the entire prize, and it is asserted rather than demonstrated: pilot-stage, no disclosed units, no run-rate. With that leg unproven, the evidence-backed reward is roughly two times, and the larger outcome lives only in a low-probability tail. This is a constructive business one disclosure away from a position, and that disclosure is not yet here.

It re-enters on a future scan if the next quarter converts the growth leg from a claim into disclosed numbers.

Screened reference levels only, not held prices and not a recommendation to buy: the review framed a screened entry near $6.67, a financing floor at $5.00, a re-rate scenario at $13, and a right-tail overshoot at $32. No position was taken.

Put the rejections side by side and the discipline is obvious. Several different reasons, not one a weak chart. The 100% elimination rate is the screen refusing to let a good label, story, company, or even a clean deep review substitute for the one thing the screen requires: an early structural force caught at an inflection happening now, in a vehicle that can survive to capture it. The eight names the screen built into this book cleared that bar; the two we added by hand last week came in on conviction and now carry the burden of earning that same work. Nothing in this week's screen did.
