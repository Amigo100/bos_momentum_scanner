📊 Market Context

The S&P 500 reached a historic milestone this week, hitting 7,000 for the first time on Wednesday before pulling back as the Federal Reserve held rates steady. The broad market index ended the day down 0.01% at 6,978.03, after touching an all-time intraday high of 7,002.28. The S&P 500 closed Thursday at 6,969.01 after declining 0.13%, while the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.72% to 23,685.12 and the Dow advanced 0.11% to 49,071.56. The Russell 2000 is up a robust 5.8% year-to-date, significantly outpacing the S&P 500's 1.8% gain for 2026.

Technology led the week's early gains, with "hyperscalers" and semiconductor giants driving the primary momentum in the record-breaking sessions. Chip stocks rallied following upbeat earnings results, with Seagate Technology jumping 19% after beating expectations and ASML reporting record orders with rosy 2026 guidance due to the AI boom. However, healthcare insurance suffered a catastrophic blow as UnitedHealth Group shares plummeted 20% after providing soft 2026 outlook, dragging down competitors like Humana and CVS Health.

The VIX briefly soared above 20 on January 20th before slipping to 17, while short-dated volatility plunged with the 1-Day Volatility Index down 5 points to 12. Microsoft dragged down markets Thursday with a roughly 10% slide - its worst day since March 2020 - after reporting slowing cloud growth and issuing soft operating margin guidance for Q3. Conversely, Meta surged 9% after calling for Q1 sales of $53.5-56.5 billion, well above the $51.41 billion consensus.

The four major hyperscalers - Microsoft, Meta, Alphabet and Amazon - are expected to boost capital expenditures this year to over $470 billion from about $350 billion in 2025. This $120 billion increase in AI infrastructure spending is creating massive bottlenecks in power grid capacity and critical materials - exactly where our GREEN signals are positioned.
