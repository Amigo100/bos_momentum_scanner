📊 Market Context

The Russell 2000 dominated headlines this week with an explosive 8.0% gain year-to-date, crushing the S&P 500's modest 1.4% advance, driven by a cooler-than-expected January CPI report released February 11 showing inflation fell to 2.4% annually, below consensus estimates of 2.5%. The S&P 500 managed marginal weekly gains of 0.12% while the Nasdaq eked out 0.15%, with the Dow closing down 0.08%, as the VIX surged 18% to settle at 20.82 amid three days of selling pressure that began Wednesday.

The dramatic sector rotation story dominated market action, with basic materials leading gains at 9.05%, followed by industrials and energy, while aggressive rotation out of the "Magnificent Seven" saw technology leaders like Apple drop 5% on Thursday alone. Software companies were hit particularly hard, with the S&P 500 software and services industry group down roughly 20% year-to-date amid rising concerns that AI advances could erode market share. Asset managers adopted "barbell" strategies, balancing AI exposures with defensive plays in utilities and healthcare, as quality emerged as the most sought-after factor.

The market's descent began Wednesday but accelerated Thursday after Cisco plummeted 12.3% on weak guidance, serving as a canary in the coal mine for enterprise technology spending. This sparked broader fears that the massive $650 billion in planned AI capital expenditures may not yield promised returns, triggering "AI capex fatigue" that saw both the S&P 500 and Nasdaq breach their 50-day moving averages, activating automated sell programs. Despite the cooling CPI data sending the 10-year Treasury yield to 4.06%, markets treated Friday's inflation relief as a "sell the news" event rather than sparking the typical "Goldilocks" rally.

The CME FedWatch Tool saw March rate cut probabilities surge above 75% following the CPI data, with markets pricing in at least three 25-basis-point cuts by year-end. This dramatic shift from 40% to over 75% March cut odds represents the market's conviction that the Fed's "higher-for-longer" stance is ending. Next week brings key economic data and potential Fed commentary, while investors should monitor the sustainability of the S&P 500's 50-day moving average and quarterly earnings from remaining AI-linked firms, as fundamental metrics like P/E ratios and free cash flow yields regain importance over speculative growth potential.
