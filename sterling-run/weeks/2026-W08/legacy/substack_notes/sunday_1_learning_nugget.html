The market punishes the fully invested and rewards the patient. Cash isn't a drag on performance — it's dry powder waiting for the right target.

Most traders think holding cash means they're "missing out" or being lazy. Wrong. Cash is a strategic position, not idle capital. It's the difference between being forced to sell a winner to fund the next opportunity versus having ammunition ready when conviction strikes.

Like keeping an emergency fund in medicine, cash reserves let you respond to market opportunities without compromising existing positions. When our screening system flashes GREEN on multiple setups simultaneously, we can deploy immediately. When it produces zero signals — which happens regularly — we don't chase mediocre setups just to feel active.

Our portfolio protocol maintains cash reserves at all times. During February's correction, while others watched their fully-invested accounts bleed, we went shopping. $NVDA at $118, $SMCI at $890, $ARM at $102 — all cleared our screening system while others were selling in panic.

A market correction becomes a shopping trip, not a crisis, when you have dry powder ready. The best opportunities often arrive when sentiment is worst and cash is scarce for most participants.
