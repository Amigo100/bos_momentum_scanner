Everyone teaches you how to buy. Almost nobody teaches you how to sell. And the sell decision is where most of the money is made or lost.

A doctor who is excellent at diagnosis but terrible at discharge planning creates a different kind of harm. Knowing when to let go — when the treatment is complete, when the condition has changed — is the other half of the skill.

Exit strategy is more important than entry strategy because it determines whether paper gains become real gains. Without a defined exit, you are relying on emotion to tell you when to sell. And emotion will always either sell too early out of fear or too late out of greed.

Our screening system generates structural exit signals that indicate when the trend has changed, independent of our opinion. Combined with trailing stop discipline, we have a two-layer exit system that handles both slow deterioration and sudden reversals.

The goal is not to sell at the top. It is to sell at a level that protects the move.
