What the Saturday Workflow Does

The saturday_workflow.py script is the single command that bridges your chat

session to the full automation stack. It:

1. Merges your chat decisions with scanner technical data → signals.json

(the format ALL downstream systems already consume)

2. Updates portfolio — adds new positions, closes exits, refreshes prices

3. Generates market analysis — API call for fresh market context (if needed)

4. Builds content schedule — content_production_guide.py reads your repo

(portfolio, themes, winners, equity curve) and assigns categories + topics

to each day of the week

5. Places newsletter — copies your chat-generated HTML to the right directories

6. Archives everything — weekly folder for historical reference

After running this, you have:

  • content_production_guide.md — attach to Claude.ai each day for Substack posts
  • newsletter.html — ready to publish to Substack
  • Updated portfolio and equity curve
  • Tweet system fed with fresh signals.json

Selectivity Tips

1. The system's edge is what it DOESN'T buy. Passing on a weak session is not

a failure — it's the system working. Capital preserved for a strong session

outperforms capital deployed into mediocre themes.

2. Theme quality > individual stock quality. A mediocre stock in a PRIME theme

outperforms a great stock in an AVOID theme. The theme provides the structural

tailwind that sustains multi-month runs. Individual merit alone produces trades,

not investments.

3. Wave alignment is the signal. When a stock rides a structural force with

measurable capital flows — validated through ETF inflows, institutional

accumulation, M&A activity, and government spending allocation — that IS the

capital flow wave your system is designed to catch. The strongest setups

combine macro force alignment (the wave is real and funded) with scanner

momentum confirmation (the timing is right). Source convergence — when the

scanner and the macro overlay independently identify the same stock — is

the most powerful signal the system can produce.

4. The macro overlay directs attention, not decisions. Prompt 2B identifies

structural forces with investable small-cap vehicles and feeds candidates

into the discovery batch. But every candidate — whether from the scanner or

the macro overlay — earns its score through the same rigorous bottom-up

analysis. Being in a powerful wave doesn't excuse weak fundamentals, bad

timing, or a fragile capital structure. The macro overlay ensures we LOOK

at stocks in the strongest waves; the analysis pipeline judges quality.

5. The bear case must target the RETURN DRIVER, not just the company. A rigorous

bear case asks "what prevents this stock from making us money?" — not just "what

could go wrong with the business?" For narrative stocks, "no revenue" is a fact,

not a bear case — the bear case is what kills the narrative. For premium-multiple

stocks, "expensive" is a description, not a bear case — the bear case is what

collapses the premium. Push back until the bear case threatens the actual mechanism

by which the stock is supposed to make money.

6. The gate screens, the DD decides. The gate is a binary PASS/FAIL filter —

it catches disqualifiers, checks the math works, identifies the bear case, and

screens timing. It does NOT produce conviction scores or position sizing. Those

decisions belong entirely to the DD (Prompt 7), where forensic depth produces

honest judgment. The deeper you dig, the more risks you find — that's the DD

working, not failing. The CRO audits whether the DD honestly reflected its findings.

7. Use the right valuation lens for the right stock. Prompt 7 builds an

independent price target BEFORE checking analyst consensus — and uses methods

matched to the stock's return driver. Peer-relative EV/Revenue for fundamental

re-rating plays. Theme trajectory and comparable precedent for narrative plays.

Growth-justified multiple analysis for premium stocks. Applying fundamental

methods to a narrative stock produces a FAIL verdict on what might be the next

RGTI. Applying narrative methods to a value stock inflates speculative upside.

The return driver (Phase 0) determines the lens. The lens determines the target.

8. Run the Theme Power Check after Prompt 4 whenever you suspect the batch is

composed of isolated micro-themes rather than a genuine capital flow wave.

9. A WATCHLIST verdict is underused. Good thesis + bad timing = WATCHLIST with a

specific pullback target, not a forced BUY at an elevated price. Track watchlist

tickers for re-evaluation when conditions change.

10. The CRO has veto power. If Prompt 8 removes a stock, it does NOT go into

new_positions in decisions.json. The Saturday workflow adds new_positions to the

portfolio automatically — there is no second check. A CRO removal that leaks

into new_positions creates an unauthorised trade.

PROMPT 2: MARKET ENVIRONMENT & STRUCTURAL LANDSCAPE CORE

When: After Prompt 1, BEFORE signals. ~10 min. Output stays in context as the reference frame for all downstream analysis.

After: → Prompt 3 (discovery) with scanner signals.

Mode: Extended Thinking (Opus 4.6 with extended thinking ON, web search ON).

This prompt requires substantial web search across economic data, capital flows,

policy developments, and structural trends. Extended thinking synthesises these

into a coherent landscape assessment. Budget approximately 12-15 searches.

Why: Every 200%+ winner rode a structural capital wave. This prompt maps those waves BEFORE you see the signals — so theme scoring has macro context.

This prompt maps the landscape BEFORE any stock analysis begins. It answers:

  • Is the macro environment supportive of small-cap growth stocks sustaining

multi-month breakouts? (Regime)

  • Where is institutional capital structurally flowing? (Capital map)
  • What structural shifts are emerging that could produce the next wave of

200-700% winners? (Emerging shifts)

  • What does the competitive landscape for capital look like — are we in

a broad bull market where many themes work, or a narrow market where

only the strongest themes sustain runs? (Selectivity signal)

Role in the new pipeline architecture:

In the redesigned pipeline, stock analysis is separated into three cognitive

stages: DISCOVERY (Prompt 3: what themes exist in our signals?), THEME

JUDGMENT (Prompt 4: are those themes powerful enough?), and STOCK ANALYSIS

(Prompt 5: are the stocks themselves exceptional?).

Prompt 2's output serves Prompt 4 specifically. When Prompt 4 evaluates

whether "autonomous defense systems" is a powerful theme, it checks whether

Prompt 2 independently identified capital flowing into defense tech. This

INDEPENDENT CONFIRMATION — macro analysis and bottom-up analysis converging

on the same theme from different directions — is the strongest signal the

system can produce. It's how NVDA would have appeared: bottom-up discovery

finds "AI inference GPU demand" as the micro-theme, Prompt 2 independently

identifies "AI infrastructure buildout" as a massive capital flow wave, and

the convergence says "this is real."

But Prompt 2 is NOT a filter. If Prompt 3 discovers a micro-theme that

Prompt 2 did NOT identify — that's potentially the highest-value finding in

the entire session. It may be a theme so early that macro analysis hasn't

caught it yet. The absence of Prompt 2 confirmation is INFORMATION (this

theme is very early or very niche), not a PENALTY.

Critical guardrail: This prompt produces a LANDSCAPE MAP, not a priority

list. It tells you where capital is flowing and what's structurally changing.

It does NOT tell you which stocks to favour. Every stock and every theme

discovered bottom-up in Prompts 3/4 earns its score on its own merits.

Prompt 2 confirmation is an additive signal that strengthens already-good

setups — never a prerequisite for advancement.

After this prompt: Paste Prompt 3 with your scanner signals. Claude will

have this landscape context in conversation memory when it discovers and maps

themes, and again when it evaluates theme quality in Prompt 4.

Think across FIVE force categories:

   expansion → autonomous systems, IRA → clean energy deployment)

   Where does demand structurally and persistently exceed supply?

   years because expanding supply takes time (building factories, training
   workforce, permitting).
   Look for: Multi-year demand ramps meeting constrained supply (not temporary
   shortages). Where are companies raising prices because they CAN, not because
   costs went up?
   (Examples: Data centre power demand vs grid capacity, skilled trades labour

   delivery model changes, payment system transitions, cybersecurity necessity,
   infrastructure modernisation requirements.
   (Examples: Healthcare moving to outpatient/ambulatory, enterprise AI workflow
   integration, infrastructure replacement cycles)

e) CAPITAL CYCLE TURNS:
   What sectors experienced over-investment, shook out weak players through a
   bear market, and are now at the early stage of recovery?
   Capital cycle recoveries are powerful because: (1) supply has been destroyed
   by the downturn, (2) survivors have pricing power, (3) institutional capital
   re-enters as fundamentals improve, and (4) the market has been burned and
   is slow to recognise the turn — which gives us entry before consensus.
   Look for: Sectors where the last 2-3 years were brutal, multiple bankruptcies
   or exits occurred, but underlying demand is now recovering.
   (Examples: Uranium sector recovery in 2023, biotech after 2022-23 bear market,
   offshore drilling recovery, specific commodity cycles)

5. TIMING: How early are we?

     market winners yet. Highest potential but longest wait. We might see scanner
     signals in 4-12 weeks if the thesis is right.
   - FIRST MOVERS: 1-3 stocks have started moving but no broad recognition yet.
     This is where our scanner might catch the first signal in the wave.
   - EARLY ADOPTION: Multiple stocks moving, first analyst coverage appearing,
     sector ETFs beginning to see inflows. Still early enough for 3-5x returns.
   - BROAD ADOPTION: Consensus forming, heavy coverage, ETF inflows established.
     Overlaps with "established waves" from Part 2. Late for new entry.
6. DURATION: Is this a 6-month trade, a 2-year theme, or a decade-long secular
   shift? Our system needs 12+ months of runway to produce monster winners.
7. SMALL-CAP SIGNAL: What type of small-cap stock would show up in our scanner

   autonomous systems software companies, defence tech hardware suppliers under
   $2B market cap.") This helps pattern-match when we see scanner signals later.

### FACTOR 2: DEMAND VISIBILITY (20% weight)

Is the demand driving this theme MEASURABLE and ACCELERATING? The best themes

FOR OPTIONALITY-REGIME THEMES:

- Funding environment: is capital flowing into this space? (VC rounds, IPOs,

FOR TRANSITION-REGIME THEMES:

  expectations?
- Customer adoption signals: are reference customers converting to contracts?
- Revenue quality: is revenue recurring/contracted or one-off/lumpy?

- 1-4: Widely covered theme. Consensus is already bullish. Heavy institutional

### FACTOR 4: TIMING & LIFECYCLE (10% weight)

- 1-4: Late cycle. Theme is well-known, heavily invested, most gains captured.

### FACTOR 5: CAPITAL CYCLE HEALTH (25% weight — HAS VETO POWER)

Is the competitive landscape in this theme supportive of outsized returns,
or is capital flooding in and destroying margins? The best themes have demand
exceeding supply with limited competition. The worst have dozens of companies
chasing the same opportunity with undifferentiated offerings.

Search: "[micro-theme] competition supply landscape new entrants"

Adapt to valuation regime:

FOR FUNDAMENTAL-REGIME THEMES:
- Are earnings revisions positive across the theme? (Rising tide)
- Is capex disciplined or is everyone over-investing? (Supply flooding in?)
- Are margins expanding or compressing? (Pricing power or margin erosion?)

FOR OPTIONALITY-REGIME THEMES:
- How many companies are pursuing this opportunity? (2-3 = healthy, 20+ = crowded)
- Is TAM big enough for multiple winners or is it winner-take-all?
- Is funding runway adequate across the theme? (Mass dilution risk if everyone
  needs to raise simultaneously)

FOR TRANSITION-REGIME THEMES:
- Are early revenues being captured by the leaders or fragmented across many?
- Is the market rewarding first-movers or punishing everyone equally?
- Are incumbents entering with superior resources? (Competitive displacement risk)

SCORING:
- 8-10: Demand exceeds supply. Limited competition. Pricing power. Companies

  fighting a war of attrition with 15 competitors.
- 5-7: Competition entering but incumbents still advantaged. Demand growth
  outpacing new supply for now, but the window may narrow over 12-18 months.
- 1-4: Overcrowded. Margin compression across the theme. Too many companies
  chasing the same TAM. Even if demand is real, returns are diluted across
  too many players.

**VETO: If Capital Cycle Health ≤ 3, cap theme classification at SELECTIVE
regardless of composite score. An overcrowded theme destroys returns no matter
