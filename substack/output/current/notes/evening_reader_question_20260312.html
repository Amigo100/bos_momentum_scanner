Reader question: "How do you decide position size when you have a mix of high-conviction and speculative names?"

Great question. We size by conviction, not by how much we like the story. Our highest-conviction positions get the largest allocation — period. Speculative names get smaller allocations regardless of how exciting the thesis is.

Here's a real example from our portfolio right now. $SOFI at $18.25 (+131% from $7.90) was a full-sized position from day one — the thesis was clear, the numbers were strong, the catalyst stack was deep. $BAND at $16.31 (-1.5% from $16.55) is a reduced-size position — compelling thesis, but newer to our universe with a key earnings test coming in April.

The math works in your favour over time. When a full-sized position like SOFI runs +131%, it moves the portfolio significantly. When a smaller speculative name pulls back, the impact is contained. You survive the speculative bets that don't work while the conviction names compound.

Risk-defined sizing means we never have to make emotional decisions about cutting positions. The position size IS the risk management.
