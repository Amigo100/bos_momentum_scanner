"Should I sell my winners to average down on my losers?"

This is one of the most common questions I get — and the answer is almost always no. Cutting winners to fund losers is one of the most destructive habits in active investing. It feels logical (lock in gains, lower your cost basis on the laggard) but it inverts the math that makes portfolios work.

Right now we hold $SOFI at +136.7% and $EVTL at -16.2%. The temptation to trim SOFI and buy more EVTL is real. But think about what that actually does: you're taking capital away from a position with confirmed momentum and putting it into a position where the original thesis is under pressure.

The better framework: let winners compound and let your exit discipline handle the losers. If EVTL hits its systematic exit level, it exits. If SOFI keeps running, it keeps running. You don't need to intervene — the system handles both scenarios.

Winners don't need your help. And losers don't need more of your capital. They need a decision: hold within your risk parameters, or exit cleanly. That's it.
