📊 Market Context

The S&P 500 hit the psychological 7,000 milestone on Wednesday but couldn't sustain momentum above that level, while major indices delivered mixed results for the week ending February 6. The S&P 500 gained 2% Friday to close the week up 0.3%, while the Russell 2000 surged 3.5% on Friday alone but still declined 2% for the week following earlier outperformance. The Nasdaq 100 and Dow Jones both declined for the week, down 0.2% and 0.4% respectively, as tech faced renewed pressure.

Healthcare emerged as the week's biggest casualty, with insurers suffering steep declines after the government proposed only a 0.09% increase in reimbursement rates for 2027 versus 5.06% for 2026, sending Humana plunging 26.8% and UnitedHealth down 19.5%. Meanwhile, Communication Services, Industrials and Health Care maintain Outperform ratings based on solid fundamentals and AI adoption potential. The Russell 2000's relative strength highlights ongoing rotation from expensive mega-caps to smaller domestic companies, with the small-cap index now up 7.5% year-to-date while the Nasdaq trades down 1%.

The Federal Reserve held rates steady at 3.50%-3.75% at its January 28 meeting, pausing after three consecutive quarter-point cuts, with two dissents from governors favoring additional easing. Fed Chair Powell expects tariff-related inflation to peak by mid-2026, stating "there's an expectation that sometime in the middle quarters of the year we'll see tariff inflation topping out." Market volatility remained elevated with the VIX at 18.0, up 10.16% for the period, reflecting ongoing uncertainty around policy transitions.

Q4 earnings season continues delivering robust results, with the S&P 500's blended earnings growth rate accelerating to 13.0% from 8.3% at quarter-end, driven by positive surprises in Communication Services, Health Care, and Financials. Key earnings this week included Alphabet's strong Q4 beat with $2.82 EPS (+31% YoY) on $113.8 billion revenue (+18% YoY), while Amazon's AWS segment grew 24% to $35.6 billion. Looking ahead, all eyes turn to NVIDIA's February 25 earnings report, which will serve as a definitive health check for the AI ecosystem and could determine whether the tech rally sustains its momentum through 2026.
