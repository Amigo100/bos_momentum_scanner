📊 Market Context

Stocks are on track for a relatively flat week, but investors may feel a little whipsawed following an early week drop and subsequent recovery on geopolitical volatility. On the week, the Russell 2000 (RTX), S&P 500 (SPX), and Dow Jones ($DJI) are on track to be slightly down, while the Nasdaq Composite is on track to be slightly higher.

Coming into this holiday-shortened week, stocks saw the largest one-day drop on Tuesday after U.S. President Donald Trump sped up his push for Greenland and threatened to impose new tariffs in response to any EU retaliations. Trump subsequently tempered his threats by stating he would not take Greenland by force and markets recovered the following day. Although the VIX has pulled back from 20 to 15 over the past four days, I'm not convinced that we are done with volatility.

The market is showing hints of a rotation early this year as small-cap companies rise and the tech sector stumbles, reversing stock market trends from 2025. Real assets are leading the charge in 2026, with basic materials posting the largest gains so far this year at 9.05%, followed by industrials and energy. In the value index, small caps are up 5.94% compared to large-cap returns growth at 2.80% so far in 2026.

The Federal Open Market Committee will meet as planned on Tuesday, January 27 and Wednesday, January 28. Despite recent disagreements among FOMC members about longer-term policy trajectory, there's virtually no chance the Fed will change rates when the meeting ends Wednesday.

Earnings season kicks off this week for tech's biggest names, with reports from Apple, Meta, Microsoft and Tesla. The four hyperscalers — Microsoft, Meta, Alphabet and Amazon — are expected to boost capital expenditures this year to over $470 billion from about $350 billion in 2025. This heightened focus on AI spending returns and earnings validation creates a critical juncture for momentum traders as sector leadership potentially shifts toward broader market participation.
