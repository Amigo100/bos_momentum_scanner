📊 Market Context

US equities delivered a mixed weekly performance with the market-cap weighted S&P 500 tracking slightly lower while the S&P 500 Equal Weight index gained 0.80% on the week, highlighting a clear divergence beneath the surface. The Russell 2000 trounced large caps by an average of 45 basis points per day over a historic 13-session outperformance streak, with the small-cap index posting a healthy 2.4% weekly gain. As of Thursday's close, the Russell 2000 sat at 2,718.77 (+0.76% daily) while the S&P 500 reached 6,933.63 (+0.46%), with the VIX settling at 15.73, down 6.92% on the day.

The market is definitively experiencing a rotation that has picked up momentum, with basic materials leading sector gains at 9.05%, followed by industrials and energy. Capital is rapidly fleeing mega-cap technology firms as investors pour funds into "old economy" pillars—specifically consumer defensive and energy sectors—as a defensive posture against geopolitical tensions and trade war threats. This bifurcation reflects the continuation of the "rotation trade" of 2026, with relative outperformance in equal-weight indices showing softness in mega-cap tech.

Strong earnings from Wall Street banks dominated the week, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley beating expectations, while JP Morgan, Bank of America, and Wells Fargo also announced earnings beats. December's payrolls report showed few signs of labor-market distress, pushing investors to hedge bets on Fed easing at upcoming meetings. The next FOMC meeting is January 27-28, with the CME FedWatch Tool estimating just a 5% chance of a quarter-percentage point cut as analysts widely expect the Fed to hold steady at the upcoming meeting, with bond futures markets seeing only 16% odds of a January cut.

The upcoming Q1 earnings season will be critical to watch, with small-cap companies projected to deliver 60% growth—if achieved, the current rotation will be viewed as the start of a multi-year bull market for domestic equities. The market will remain hypersensitive to Federal Reserve leadership news and European tariff formalization, making the S&P 500 Equal-Weighted Index a better gauge of market health than the tech-heavy cap-weighted version. The "quality cyclical" trade has become the dominant theme, with domestic manufacturing and financial sectors as primary beneficiaries, while market health will be measured by breadth of participation across the Russell 2000 and S&P 400.
