Week 5: 2 GREEN Signals | Small-Cap Surge Drives New Momentum

The Russell 2000's 7% YTD surge vs S&P 500's 2% signals a fundamental shift - and our scanner is capturing the rotation

📊 Market Context

U.S. markets delivered a mixed week as momentum trading dynamics shifted beneath the surface. The S&P 500 closed up 1.8% year-to-date, while the small-cap Russell 2000 surged 5.8% year-to-date, signaling a clear divergence from 2025's large-cap dominance. The Russell 2000 has gained over 7% so far this year compared to just over 2% for the S&P 500, with the small-cap index outperforming for each of the first 14 trading days. The VIX increased 3.2% to 16, reflecting modest anxiety as investors digested earnings and Fed policy shifts.

A pronounced sector rotation accelerated this week, with cyclical areas leading while technology lagged. Basic materials sector posted the largest gains at 9.05%, followed by industrials and energy. Tech stocks hit a slump this year after strong 2025 AI-driven earnings growth, with the market showing clear rotation as small-cap companies rise and tech stumbles. Energy, financials, consumer discretionary, materials, and industrials repeatedly led trading, while small- and mid-cap stocks outperformed their large-cap peers.

The Federal Reserve's Wednesday decision dominated market psychology this week. The FOMC maintained rates at 3.5%-3.75% as expected, with two dissenting votes preferring a cut. Powell stated "The U.S. economy expanded at a solid pace last year and is coming into 2026 on a firm footing", while describing the current federal funds rate as "loosely neutral" with committee members finding it "hard to look at the data and say that policy is significantly restrictive". Major earnings also moved individual names, with companies including American Express, Chevron, Exxon Mobil, and Verizon reporting results.

Looking ahead, momentum stock traders face a critical inflection point. Markets expect the Fed to cut once or twice this year, most likely in June and December according to futures pricing. President Trump's nomination of Kevin Warsh to replace Powell when his term ends in May adds leadership uncertainty, with markets sliding on the announcement. The ongoing small-cap outperformance streak creates compelling opportunities for momentum traders willing to pivot from mega-cap growth into domestic cyclical plays, particularly as earnings growth gaps between technology and the rest of the market close, broadening this rally.
